# TS ACADEMY – CYBERSECURITY
## CLASS 003 NOTES

### 1. Understanding a Cyberattack

Four important elements:

1. **Threat Actor:** The person responsible for carrying out an attack.
2. **Vulnerability:** A weakness that an attacker can exploit.
3. **Pathway/Delivery Method:** How the attacker reaches the victim or system.
4. **Mechanism of Action:** How the attack works and what it does.

**Example – Phishing and Ransomware:**
- Threat actor: Attacker pretending to be a co-worker.
- Vulnerability: Employee's lack of awareness.
- Pathway: Phishing email.
- Mechanism: Ransomware affects the victim's system, potentially preventing access to files.

### 2. Main Cybersecurity Threat Categories

**A. Unauthorized Access**

When someone accesses a system, account or network without permission.

Example: A hospital visitor accessing the staff network without authorization.

**B. System Design Error**

A weakness or flaw built into a system during its design or development.

Example: A newly manufactured TV having a recurring fault due to a design problem.

**C. Denial of Service (DoS)**

An attack where a single source floods a server with requests until the service becomes unavailable.

**D. Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)**

An attack where multiple sources send excessive traffic to one target, making its service unavailable.

**Difference:**
- DoS: One source.
- DDoS: Multiple sources.

### 3. Botnets and IoT Devices

**IoT:** Internet of Things. Devices connected to the internet.

Examples:
- Smart TVs
- Smart refrigerators
- Smart microwaves
- CCTV cameras
- Baby monitors

**Botnet:** A group of compromised devices controlled by an attacker.

Attackers can use botnets to send large amounts of traffic to a target, causing DDoS attacks.

**Possible signs of malware or compromise:**
- Unusual system lag.
- Unexpected resource usage.
- Unknown programs appearing briefly.
- Storage filling up unexpectedly.
- Emails appearing in the sent folder that the owner did not send.

Note: These signs can have other causes and do not automatically prove malware infection.

### 5. Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

**Scenario:** An online shop accepts card payments through its website but has no HTTPS security certificate.

**Step 1 – Identify the Risk**
- Asset: Customer card details.
- Threat: Fraudsters and cyberattackers.
- Vulnerability: No HTTPS.
- Risk: Card details could be intercepted and stolen.

**Step 2 – Assess Likelihood**
- Are online shops being targeted? Yes.
- Is the data easy to intercept? Yes.
- Likelihood: HIGH.

**Step 3 – Assess Impact**
- Customer information exposed? Yes.
- Legal or financial damage? Yes.
- Loss of customer trust? Yes.
- Impact: HIGH.

**Step 4 – Calculate Risk Level**

Risk = Likelihood × Impact

HIGH × HIGH = HIGH RISK.

**Step 5 – Risk Management**
- Install an HTTPS certificate.
- Use a trusted payment provider.
- Monitor suspicious transactions.

### 6. Career Paths in Cybersecurity

**A. GRC Analyst**

GRC means:
- Governance
- Risk
- Compliance

Typical daily activities:
1. Identify risks.
2. Assess threats.
3. Take appropriate action.
4. Evaluate the outcome.

**B. Red Team – Penetration Tester**

A penetration tester performs authorized simulated attacks to identify system weaknesses.

Typical daily activities:
1. Target – Identify the system to test.
2. Find – Discover vulnerabilities.
3. Attack Simulated – Perform authorized security tests.
4. Action – Document findings and recommend fixes.
5. Outcome – Report results.

### 7. Ethics in Cybersecurity

Ethical principles every cybersecurity professional must follow:

1. **Confidentiality:** Keep sensitive information private.
2. **Responsible Disclosure:** Report vulnerabilities responsibly.
3. **Legal Boundaries:** Obtain authorization before testing systems.
4. **Do No Harm:** Avoid damaging systems or data.
5. **Accountability:** Take responsibility for your actions.
6. **Scope:** Work within agreed testing boundaries.
7. **Respect Privacy:** Protect personal information.
8. **Professional Standards:** Maintain honesty and integrity.

**KEY LESSON:** A cybersecurity professional must have technical skills, responsibility and good ethical standards.
